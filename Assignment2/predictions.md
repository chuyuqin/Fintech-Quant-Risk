# Preliminary predictions

The predictions below are based on descriptive statistics, observation counts, and plots. The report compares them with the fitted results.

1. Pairwise correlations can be indefinite because entries use different observations; complete-case correlations are Gram matrices and hence PSD. IDX nearly replicates the weighted basket, so the true correlation has a near-zero eigenvalue and is vulnerable to small inconsistent pairwise errors. D pairs (83-96 observations) are least reliable; C-D has the fewest (83). Only 81 complete rows remain.
2. The return plot suggests approximately the final 40 days form a higher-volatility regime. Predicted ascending 5% VaR: fitted t, historical, equally weighted normal, EW normal 0.97, EW normal 0.94. The first three are a tentative ordering: a t can fit the narrow center and push mass beyond the 5% cutoff; historical reflects mostly the calm regime. Faster EW responds more strongly to the recent jump. Excess kurtosis 2.13 with approximately zero skew is inconsistent with a typical 500-observation single normal regime, though not logically impossible.
3. A has 406 losses below -20%, B 393, and their union 782 of 10,000. Diversification therefore crosses the 5% default boundary and should raise 5% VaR. I predict lower ES for the diversified $2m position because most tail defaults affect only half the investment (union counts alone cannot establish severity). Strong negative skew and excess kurtosis above 22 make normal VaR inappropriate.
4. X1 looks normal; X2 and X3 suggest t margins. X2's excess kurtosis falls from 7.08 to 1.71 after removing row 952, so the strength of that evidence is sensitive to one observation. Rank plots show corner clustering and joint tail counts 6-12 versus 0.625 under independence. Predict a t copula, while recognizing positive Gaussian correlation can also generate more joint extremes than independence.
5. A positive omitted industry residual covariance adds 2*wA*wB*cov(eA,eB) to variance. Dropping it should understate P1 risk and overstate P2 risk, since P2 has a negative weight product.
